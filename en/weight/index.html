Weight and BMI

The short answer: A BMI of 18.5–25 was linked to the lowest risk. In men, overweight was linked to a 12% higher risk, and grade 2 obesity to a risk 2.68 times higher.

The link between weight and mortality looks like a U: risk is lowest in the healthy BMI range and rises on both sides, especially with obesity. The data here come from a pooled analysis of 239 cohort studies with over 10 million people, so they are among the best established in the field.

What the data say

The Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (Lancet 2016) analyzed people who never smoked and had no chronic disease, excluding the first five years of follow-up. Compared with a BMI of 18.5 to 25, the risk of death from any cause was: in men 1.12 with overweight, and 1.70, 2.68 and 4.24 with grade 1, 2 and 3 obesity. In women: 1.08, 1.37, 1.86 and 2.73.

What this means in years of life

A hazard ratio is not years. It multiplies the risk of dying at each age, and converting it to years depends on age, sex and other factors. That is why the calculator does it for you: change the weight and see the difference. Any figure in years is the model's estimate, not a result reported in a study.

Risk falls with age

For every 5 BMI units above 25, risk rose by 51% in men and 30% in women. By age (combined estimate): 1.52 at 35–49, 1.37 at 50–69 and 1.21 at 70–89. The effect is stronger at a younger age, so it pays to pay attention early.

Being too light also carries risk

At a BMI below 18.5 the risk was 1.83 in men and 1.53 in women. Part of this comes from illness that causes weight loss, so someone losing weight without a clear reason needs a check-up, not a compliment.

Limits of BMI

BMI does not distinguish fat from muscle, and does not show where the fat is. Waist circumference adds information, which is why it is also in the calculator. All the numbers here come from observational studies, not from a trial in which people lost weight.

Myth or fact

"A little extra weight always protects"What do we know?

What we know: In a group with no disease and no smoking, even mild overweight was linked to higher risk (1.12 in men).

"BMI is a perfect measure"What do we know?

What we know: It is crude. Waist circumference, blood sugar and blood pressure complete the picture.
