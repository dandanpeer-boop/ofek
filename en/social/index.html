Social ties

This may be the most surprising finding in the field: strong social ties are linked to a 50% higher likelihood of survival. In a review of 148 studies, the strength of the link was similar to well-known risk factors such as smoking and exercise.

3 min read · 3 sources

50%higher

likelihood of survival with strong social ties, in 148 studies [1]

308,849participants

in Holt-Lunstad's review (PLoS Medicine 2010) [1]

What was measured

The link was strongest when broad social involvement was measured: friends, family, community and shared activity. Living alone, by itself, showed a much weaker link. In other words, what matters is the quality and depth of relationships, not just who lives with you.

Isolation and loneliness are not the same thing

Isolation is an objective state: few meetings and conversations with people. Loneliness is a feeling: you can be surrounded by people and still feel alone. In a meta-analysis of 90 studies with 2.2 million participants (Wang, Nature Human Behaviour 2023), social isolation was linked to a 32% higher risk of death, and loneliness to 14%. Both matter, but isolation showed the stronger link.

Loneliness is a public health issue

In 2023 the US Surgeon General issued an advisory on the "epidemic of loneliness", recommending that social connection be treated as part of public health, like nutrition and physical activity.

Myth or fact

"If you live with a partner, you're not lonely"What do we know?

What we know: In the research, living with someone was the weakest measure. What matters is the depth of relationships.
