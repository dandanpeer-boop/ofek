Nutrition

The short answer: There is no magic food: the leading dietary causes of death were too much salt, too few whole grains and too little fruit, and a Mediterranean diet with olive oil or nuts cut heart and stroke events by 30% in the PREDIMED trial.

According to the Global Burden of Disease study, poor diet was linked to 11 million deaths worldwide in 2017. The top three factors were not "forbidden foods" but simple things: too much salt, too few whole grains and too little fruit.

The basis: vegetables and fruit

The World Health Organization recommends at least 400 grams of vegetables and fruit a day, about five servings. In Aune's meta-analysis (2017), each additional 200 grams a day was linked to about 10% lower mortality, with the benefit continuing up to about 800 grams a day.

Whole grains instead of refined

In a meta-analysis of 45 studies, each 90 grams of whole grains a day, for example two slices of wholemeal bread and a bowl of cereal, was linked to 17% lower mortality risk. No such link was found for refined grains or white rice.

The Mediterranean direction

In the PREDIMED trial, people at high cardiovascular risk who were given a Mediterranean diet with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts had fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths (hazard ratios 0.69 and 0.72) than on a low-fat diet. It is one of the few controlled nutrition trials with hard outcomes like these.

Salt

The World Health Organization recommends less than 5 grams of salt a day, about a teaspoon. Most salt comes from processed food, bread, cheese and sauces, not from the salt shaker. In many countries, front-of-pack labels flag products high in sodium.

Myth or fact

"There is one superfood that extends life"What do we know?

What we know: Studies show the effect of a whole dietary pattern over time, not of a single food.

"All fats are harmful"What do we know?

What we know: In PREDIMED, adding olive oil and nuts was actually linked to fewer heart events than a low-fat diet.
