Stress and mind

The short answer: Psychological distress was linked to a higher risk of dying, even at low levels, so it is worth seeking help early. ERAN, 1201, is available free and anonymously around the clock.

Psychological distress is linked to physical health. In Russ's study (BMJ 2012), the risk of death rose step by step as distress got higher, even at low levels. It is a statistical association, but it is enough to take mental health seriously.

What the research says

The link between distress and mortality is observational: distress can also be a sign of existing illness. So we can't conclude that reducing stress alone will add years. But long-term stress harms sleep, diet and movement, and those are proven factors.

Tools from the World Health Organization guide

The guide "Doing What Matters in Times of Stress" offers short skills: grounding (stop, feel your feet on the floor, breathe slowly and notice your surroundings), unhooking from distressing thoughts, and choosing one small action a day based on what matters to you.

When to ask for help

If stress lasts for weeks or affects your sleep or functioning, talk to your doctor or a mental health professional. In the US you can call or text 988; in the UK, Samaritans are on 116 123. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in Europe).

Myth or fact

"Asking for help is a sign of weakness"What do we know?

What we know: Reaching out early is one of the most practical things you can do for your health.
