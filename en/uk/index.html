Life expectancy in the United Kingdom

The short answer: According to the ONS, life expectancy at birth in the UK for 2022 to 2024 was 79.1 years for males and 83.0 years for females.

Life expectancy is a group average, not a forecast for one person. These figures come from the Office for National Statistics (ONS) and describe a three-year period.

The latest figures

The ONS release National life tables, life expectancy in the UK: 2022 to 2024 (published 10 December 2025) gives life expectancy at birth of 79.1 years for males and 83.0 years for females. At age 65 it is 18.7 more years for males and 21.2 for females.

Three-year periods and single years

The ONS publishes three-year period tables. For 2024 alone, life expectancy at birth was 79.4 years for males and 83.3 for females. A single year is noisier, which is why the ONS uses three years for its main figures.

Compared with 2019 to 2021

For 2019 to 2021 the ONS figures were 78.7 years for males and 82.7 for females at birth, and 18.3 and 20.8 more years at 65. The ONS describes the gains since then as 21 weeks for males and 18 weeks for females at birth.

What a period life table means

The ONS notes that period life tables do not give a true estimate of how long someone can expect to live on average. They show what would happen if today's death rates stayed the same for a person's whole life.

What this does not tell you

A national average ignores your own habits, test results and family history. To see what the number looks like for you, the calculator starts from a life table for your age and sex and adjusts it to your habits and tests.

Myth or fact

"Life expectancy at birth is the age you will die"What do we know?

What we know: It is a group average from a period table, and the ONS itself says it is not a true estimate for any one person.

"One year of data tells the whole story"What do we know?

What we know: The ONS uses three-year periods for its main figures because single years vary more.
