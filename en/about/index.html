About the site

How it works

The guides

Every guide is based on studies from medical journals and on guidelines from bodies such as the World Health Organization. Every number has a link to its source, and the numbers were checked against the study's abstract. When a study only shows a link and does not prove a cause (an observational study), it says so.

The calculator

The starting point is the World Health Organization's mortality table by country, age and sex. Each risk factor, for example smoking, is turned into a hazard ratio: a number from a large study that says by how much the factor multiplies or reduces the risk of dying at any age. The effect weakens in older age. Factors that are related to each other are counted with a smaller weight, so that the same thing is not counted twice. The result is shown as a range, because nobody can know a date.

Limits of the model

  • The model combines findings from separate studies, and there is no single study behind it that was tested against real data. It is not a validated model.
  • Factors that are related to each other, such as weight, sugar and blood pressure, partly affect the same thing. How the calculator handles this is an approximation, and it can overstate or understate, especially for someone with many risk factors.
  • The mortality table reflects today's mortality, not future improvements in medicine. So the estimate for young people is probably conservative, meaning too low.
  • The range shown reflects how much people with the same profile can differ from one another. It does not show the uncertainty of the model itself.
  • Most studies are observational: they show a link, and it is not always possible to know how much of it is cause and effect. So "how many years you will gain" is an estimate, not a promise.
  • Many of the studies were carried out in Western countries. The model does not know about rare diseases, genetics or acute, meaning sudden, risks.
  • In a preliminary check we ran on US NHANES data (about 23,500 adults, 10-year follow-up) the model separated high-risk from low-risk people well, but on average predicted about 15 to 20 percent more deaths than were observed. We therefore added a calibration: the combined risk ratio is multiplied by 0.75, gradually according to the number of factors entered (no calibration up to two factors, full calibration from eight factors). The calibration was estimated on the same data, so it is not an independent check; it has not been reviewed by an actuary and was not done on an Israeli population. It raises the estimate by roughly 0.5 to 1 year for someone who entered four factors, and by about 2 years for someone who entered many, so the absolute estimate is less certain than the comparison between habits.

What is not included in the number

  • A family history of breast, ovarian or colorectal cancer, diabetes or Alzheimer's appears in the notes only. It mostly concerns the personal risk of the disease itself.
  • A parent who died young (before age 70) from a non-external cause is not counted. The calculator only counts parental longevity. The estimate may be slightly optimistic.
  • The number of cigarettes and the years of smoking are not asked, and the calculator assumes an average smoker.
  • The number is life expectancy, not healthy life expectancy and not quality of life.
  • Blood test values older than 24 months are not included, and biological age is calculated only from tests taken within 12 months.

Who checked it

Every number was checked against the abstract or the full text of the study, and this is stated next to each source. No doctor or actuary has reviewed it yet. For cross-checking we also used several artificial intelligence models, which is not a substitute for human review.

Ongoing updates

Every week PubMed, the database of medical articles, is scanned for new studies on mortality and life expectancy: meta-analyses (a summary of several studies), controlled trials and large follow-up studies. Every study found is checked against its abstract and against the numbers already on the site. Only a strong study that changes the picture updates a number in the calculator or a guide. Not every new study changes anything, and that is intentional: a single unusual study should not move your estimate.

Privacy

Your answers are not sent anywhere. The calculation and the reading of blood-test files and watch files all happen in your browser. If you choose to remember your data, it is stored only in this browser, and you can delete it at any moment with the "Delete my data" button at the bottom of the page. To know how many people visit the site and which pages, we use Google Analytics. It receives the page address and general details such as device type and country, not what you entered in the calculator. You can check for yourself: once the page has loaded, disconnect from the internet or switch to airplane mode, and the calculator keeps working, even with a blood-test file.

Who built this site

The site was built by a private individual. There is no insurance company or supplement company behind it, and it has no ads. The calculator and the guides are free and will stay free. We are checking whether there is interest in a small paid personal document, but it does not exist yet and we charge nothing.

The reason: after my mother died at 77, and cancer was found in my father at 77, I decided to help people improve their life expectancy.

The site was written with the help of artificial intelligence tools. Every number was checked against the abstract or full text of the original study.

What it is not

It is not a diagnosis and not medical advice. The studies describe averages of large groups, not the future of any one person. For any question about your health, talk to your doctor.
