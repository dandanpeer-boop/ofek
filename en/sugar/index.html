Sugar

Excess sugar does harm in two ways: through what we eat and drink, and through blood sugar levels. Prediabetes is a stage where it can still be stopped, and in the largest trial in the field, lifestyle change cut the risk of developing diabetes by more than half.

How much is "free sugar"

The World Health Organization recommends that free sugars make up less than 10% of energy intake, and preferably less than 5%. At 2,000 calories a day, 10% is about 50 grams, roughly 12 teaspoons. Free sugars include honey, syrups and fruit juices too, not just white sugar.

Drinks are the easy place to start

In Malik's meta-analysis (2010) of more than 310,000 people, those who usually drank 1–2 sugary drinks a day had a 26% higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes than those who hardly drank any.

Prediabetes: it can be stopped

Fasting glucose of 100 to 125 mg/dL, or HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4%, is the prediabetes range. In the ERFC study, above 100 mg/dL mortality risk rose by about 10% per 18 mg/dL, and diabetes itself was linked to 80% higher risk.

In the DPP trial, 3,234 people with high blood sugar were given a program aiming for 7% weight loss and 150 minutes of activity a week. The risk of developing diabetes fell by 58%, more than with medication.

Myth or fact

"Brown sugar, honey or date syrup are healthier"What do we know?

What we know: By the World Health Organization's definition they are all free sugars and count towards the same limit.

"Prediabetes is already diabetes, nothing can be done"What do we know?

What we know: In the DPP trial, lifestyle change cut the risk of developing diabetes by 58%.
