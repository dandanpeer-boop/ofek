Five habits, more than a decade of life

In a Harvard study that followed more than 120,000 people for up to 34 years, women at age 50 who adopted all five habits were projected to live 14 years longer than those who adopted none. For men: 12.2 years.

  1. 1Don't smoke
  2. 2BMI between 18.5 and 24.9
  3. 330 minutes of activity a day
  4. 4Alcohol in moderation only
  5. 5A high-quality diet

Source: Li 2018: healthy lifestyle habits and life expectancy (Circulation)

The main tool

A personal life expectancy calculator

It starts from the World Health Organization life table for your age, sex and country, and adjusts it for dozens of factors from large studies. The result is a range, not a date, with a breakdown of what adds and what takes away years.

  • Blood tests from a fileUpload a PDF of your results and the values fill in by themselves
  • Import from a Garmin watchResting heart rate and VO2max from your watch's file
  • A "what if" simulatorMove a slider and see how many years a given change is worth
  • Completely privateThe whole calculation runs in your browser; no data is sent to any server

Myth or fact

What everybody knows, and what the research actually found

Tap a card to see the answer.

Sleep"You can "catch up" on sleep at the weekend"What do we know?

What we know: Catching up helps you feel better, but irregular sleep disrupts the body clock. A steady amount every night is better.

To the guide: Sleep →
Nutrition"There is one superfood that extends life"What do we know?

What we know: Studies show the effect of a whole dietary pattern over time, not of a single food.

To the guide: Nutrition →
Intermittent fasting"Fasting extends life, it's proven"What do we know?

What we know: Proven in animals. In humans there is no long-term study of life expectancy yet.

To the guide: Intermittent fasting →
Sugar"Brown sugar, honey or date syrup are healthier"What do we know?

What we know: By the World Health Organization's definition they are all free sugars and count towards the same limit.

To the guide: Sugar →
Movement and fitness"You need 10,000 steps, or it's not worth it"What do we know?

What we know: Over age 60 the benefit levels off around 6,000–8,000 steps, and there is benefit well before that.

To the guide: Movement and fitness →
Stress and mind"Asking for help is a sign of weakness"What do we know?

What we know: Reaching out early is one of the most practical things you can do for your health.
