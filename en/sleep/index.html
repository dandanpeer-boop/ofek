Sleep

Sleep is not wasted time. Adults need at least 7 hours a night, regularly, and the link between short sleep and mortality shows up in large studies. The good news: chronic sleep problems have an effective treatment without medication.

How many hours

According to the consensus statement of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the Sleep Research Society (2015), a healthy adult needs at least 7 hours of sleep a night. 6 hours or less is not enough to maintain health and safety.

In Cappuccio's meta-analysis (2010), short sleep was linked to 12% higher mortality risk, and very long sleep to 30%. Very long sleep is often a sign of illness rather than its cause, so this is not a recommendation to sleep less.

Regularity matters as much as quantity

A fixed wake-up time, weekends included, is the anchor of the body clock. If you want to go to bed earlier, it is better to shift gradually, a quarter of an hour at a time.

When sleep won't come

According to the American College of Physicians guideline (2016), the first treatment for chronic insomnia is cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT-I), before medication. It is available face to face, in a group, through an app or from a book. Loud snoring, pauses in breathing or severe daytime tiredness are a reason to see your doctor.

Myth or fact

"You can "catch up" on sleep at the weekend"What do we know?

What we know: Catching up helps you feel better, but irregular sleep disrupts the body clock. A steady amount every night is better.

"A sleeping pill is the answer to insomnia"What do we know?

What we know: The professional guideline recommends starting with cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT-I), before medication.
