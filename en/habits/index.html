Smoking and alcohol

The short answer: Smokers lose more than 10 years of life, and quitting at 45–54 gives back about 6. For alcohol, the threshold above which mortality risk rises is about 100 grams a week.

Smoking is the biggest risk factor you can change: smokers lose more than a decade. But it is never too late to quit, and even people who quit at 50 gain back many years.

Smoking: it's never too late

In Jha's study (NEJM 2013) of more than 200,000 Americans, smokers lost more than 10 years of life. Those who quit at 25–34 gained about 10 years, at 35–44 about 9 years, and at 45–54 about 6 years, compared with those who continued. Quitting before 40 removed about 90% of the excess risk.

Doctors, pharmacies and national quitlines offer support and stop-smoking medication. Combining support with medication raises the chance of success.

Alcohol: less is better

In an analysis of about 600,000 drinkers (Wood, Lancet 2018), the lowest mortality risk was at up to about 100 grams of pure alcohol a week, roughly 6 glasses of wine of 175 ml. People who drank more than 350 grams a week lost about 4–5 years of life expectancy at age 40.

The Global Burden of Disease study (2018) concluded that the level that minimizes overall health harm is zero. There is no reason to start drinking "for your health".

Myth or fact

"After 30 years of smoking there's no point quitting"What do we know?

What we know: Even quitting at 45–54 gains about 6 years.

"A glass of wine a day is good for the heart"What do we know?

What we know: The risk of stroke, heart failure and high blood pressure rises with any amount. The level that minimizes overall harm is zero.
